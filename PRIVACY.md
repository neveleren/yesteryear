# Yesteryear privacy policy

Last updated: November 11, 2026

Yesteryear is a browser extension that shows Google, YouTube and the web as they were in a year you choose. It has
no accounts, no servers of its own, no analytics and no ads. Nothing is sold or shared for any other purpose.

## What stays on your computer
- Your settings (the chosen year, the switches in the extension's window, the tour's choices).
- Caches that make the time machine faster: which archived copy was found for a page, the date each site was first
  archived, the period Google logos, which YouTube video plays which song, your place in the year's playlist and the
  volume.

They are kept in your browser's extension storage and are removed when you remove the extension.

## What leaves your computer, and why
Only what a feature needs, sent straight to the service that provides it:

- **Internet Archive (archive.org, web.archive.org).** When you open a search result, its address is sent to the
  Internet Archive's lookup service to find the copy from your chosen year, and that copy then loads from
  web.archive.org. On a Google results page, the names of the result sites (for example `example.com`) are sent
  to the Internet Archive to check whether those sites existed in that year. The period Google logos, Google's old
  services and the "Today in" front pages also load from the Internet Archive.
- **Google and YouTube.** Your searches go to Google and YouTube as usual; the extension only adds a date limit to
  them and changes how the page looks. To play the year's songs, the extension searches YouTube for the song titles
  of the Billboard Year-End chart (song names, nothing about you) and plays them in YouTube's embedded player.
- **Wikimedia (upload.wikimedia.org).** The pictures of the year's memes load from Wikimedia.

These services handle the requests under their own privacy policies.
